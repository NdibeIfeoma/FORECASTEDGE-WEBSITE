Most small retailers order stock on gut feel — because real forecasting looked out of reach.

Demand forecasting has always required a statistician on staff. That's expensive, slow to hire for, and hard to justify until you're already big. So most small e-commerce sellers guess, and the guesses cost real money.

01

Cash tied up in overstock

Without a real demand model, reorder decisions default to instinct — locking up cash in products that sell slowly, or not at all.

02

Stockouts during your best-selling weeks

Underordering a popular SKU means lost sales exactly when demand is highest — and customers who don't come back to check again.

03

No sense of how much buffer is enough

A single-number reorder guess hides the real risk. You're never sure if you're ordering too much "just in case," or not enough.

How a ForecastEdge reorder forecast actually gets built

No black box. Every forecast is built on established statistical methods, and every output tells you not just what we predict, but how sure we are.

01

Connect your store

Link your Shopify or WooCommerce store (or upload a sales export). Historical per-SKU sales history — you keep ownership of your data at every step.

02

We model each product's demand

Time-series analysis fitted to each SKU's own pattern — seasonality, trend, and promotional spikes — not a generic template applied to every product.

03

Get a reorder number with a confidence range

You receive a plain-language "order this many units" figure plus the statistical range it's likely to fall within — so you know how much buffer to actually carry.

Built on methods statisticians actually trust

  • Time series decomposition separates real trend and seasonality from noise, instead of chasing last month's blip.
  • Regression modeling accounts for the outside factors that move your numbers — pricing, marketing spend, seasonality.
  • Confidence intervals on every output — a forecast is a range, and we show you the range, not just the midpoint.
  • Back-tested against your own history before it's ever used to predict your future.
